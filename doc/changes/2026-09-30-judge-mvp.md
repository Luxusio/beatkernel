# Instant/Hold judging and four-control console

BeatKernel now connects compiled objects to unchanged bound input through
`JudgeEngine`, caller `Rule` registrations and Instant/Hold evaluators.
Validated profiles provide unique grade IDs and inclusive asymmetric windows,
nested narrow to wide. Both input and advance take explicitly mapped song time,
apply the signed offset once and reject overflow or backward chronology before
library-owned state changes. Equal times and negative preroll are valid; expiry
is strictly after the late boundary. Closest-target selection breaks ties by
target then ObjectId; earliest-target and custom candidate/grading policies
remain available.

Fresh Down selects at most one pending builtin start per bound destination.
Repeat and duplicate Down do not create builtin presses. Custom evaluators own
typed start acceptance through explicit eligibility and a separate pending
index, including Up and samples outside builtin windows; candidate selection
still chooses one start. A Hold requires a strictly later end and
acquires device, physical-control and logical-control ownership on its graded
head. Owner Up grades an inclusive tail or breaks an early release; expiry
misses a tail without regrab or automatic perfect. Head and tail results remain
separate for caller scoring. Ordered results retain original input metadata;
timeouts have no fabricated input. Builtin evaluators ignore nonbutton samples,
while custom evaluator/active-interaction callbacks receive typed input.

Run `cargo run -p beatkernel --example judge -- --help` for the fixed chart and
stdin grammar, or `cargo run -p beatkernel --example judge -- --fixture` for the
labeled synthetic transcript. No arguments also prints help. `--stdin` accepts
`host_ns lane down|up|repeat` with lanes 1..4, nondecreasing signed i64 host times
at or after origin 1,000,000,000 ns, and ignored blank lines. Virtual input,
binding and Transport map origin to song zero. The chart uses lane 1 Instant at
500 ms, lane 2 Hold 500..1,500 ms, lane 3 Instant at 1,000 ms and lane 4 Instant
at 1,500 ms; grades are inclusive +/-20 ms and +/-100 ms with zero offset.
Malformed or out-of-order input exits nonzero with a line number. EOF advances
to at least song 1,600,000,001 ns and prints remaining misses as `input=none`.

Twenty targeted judge tests pass in debug and release, with strict core
all-target Clippy. Targeted console checks passed help, identical fixture output
twice and ten stdin error/boundary/EOF cases. Phase 5 passed independent review
and CLI QA.
Custom-start regressions verify Up and axis samples outside builtin windows
reach the predicate, resolver and callback without flattening their payloads.
An example unit test verifies invalid UTF-8 on line 2 retains its line number.
The [judge contract](../kernel/REQ__judge.md) defines the public behavior.

## Known ceiling

Judging is single-owner and forward-only, and setup, dispatch
and results can allocate — add replay/snapshot restoration in Phase 10 and
revisit allocation only for a concrete bounded runtime requirement. Extension
callbacks are trusted and infallible; their panics and external side effects
are outside library atomic-error guarantees. The console measures no native
input or hardware latency. Full Phases 7–15 remain required, including native
gameplay/audio integration, WASAPI shared/exclusive and licensing-resolved
optional ASIO, projection, replay, generalization, Linux/macOS backends,
parsers, FFI and optimization, as specified in the
[primary goals](../common/REQ__project__primary-goals.md) and
[original plan](../../plan.md).
