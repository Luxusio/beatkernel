# Supplied-pair integer clock calibration

AffineClockMapper belongs to the OS-independent time module. It consumes caller-supplied ClockPoint observation pairs; it does not read clocks, collect hardware samples, estimate physical latency, reset devices or synchronize audio restart. Live and replay judging remain the same JudgeEngine. Windows device/QPC pairs and macOS logical-output/mach presentation pairs can be represented without equating domain IDs or inventing a common origin.

Two anchors must use the same two distinct domains and strictly increase in both domains. The slope is the reduced positive rational target-span/source-span, and the first anchor defines the offset. Original anchors remain inspectable. All timestamp differences, rational scaling and additions use checked integer arithmetic; only representable signed nanosecond timestamps are returned. Ordinary products use checked i128, with a bounded integer division fallback for wide products whose quotient remains representable. No floating-point interpolation or accumulating transport/seek anchors are used.

Every calibration has an explicit finite inclusive validity interval in its source domain. The interval must lie within the anchor span unless the caller explicitly opts into finite before/after extrapolation limits. The inverse domain interval is the rounded image of those endpoints. Unknown domain pairs and out-of-validity queries fail explicitly; the ClockMapper trait converts these detailed failures to None. Identity queries for a recognized domain also obey the finite interval. A one-anchor exact-offset constructor is only for caller-established identical-rate clocks with a genuinely exact relation, never for merely observed hardware timestamps.

Forward scaling and inverse scaling truncate signed deltas toward zero around the first anchor. Both original anchors map exactly whenever their source points are within the supplied validity interval. Because integer quantization is not bijective, inverse mapping is not an exact round trip for arbitrary unequal-rate clocks. The inverse result is clamped to the declared source interval at rounded endpoint boundaries; this keeps the inverse valid when several source nanoseconds collapse to one target nanosecond. For a forward/inverse source round trip the absolute error is conservatively bounded by ceil(source-span/target-span) + 1 nanoseconds. Exact-offset mappings round trip exactly. Callers can inspect both intervals and the reduced rate rather than assuming sample-perfect inverses.

Uncertainty is explicitly supplied by the caller: observation_error bounds the target-domain error assigned to the supplied observation pair/model, and optional residual_drift_error bounds remaining affine-model error over the entire chosen validity interval, including any permitted extrapolation. These are application-provided estimates/assumptions, not measured or guaranteed by the core. If the drift estimate is absent, quality is Unknown; extrapolation does not gain a physical guarantee. When both bounds exist, quality conservatively includes their sum, rational quantization and conversion into the inverse source domain, and reports the larger bidirectional bound as Estimated. An unrepresentable uncertainty bound reports Unknown. Hardware-observed pairs never report Exact, even when their supplied estimated error is zero and slope is one. The exact-offset constructor can report Exact because it requires a caller-established exact unit-rate relation and introduces no rational rounding.

Negative uncertainty/extrapolation bounds, mismatched domains, unordered anchors, reversed intervals, unsupported extrapolation and arithmetic overflow fail explicitly. All operations are allocation-free after construction, contain no unsafe code and have finite arithmetic work. Authored regression fixtures and buildability checks accompany implementation; tests, formal review/QA and physical device synchronization remain deferred under the user's current instruction.
